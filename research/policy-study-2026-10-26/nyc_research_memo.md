# NYC policy evidence — editorial memo
As of 26 October 2026. Machine-readable evidence and citations: `nyc_policies.json`.

## Recommended four-case visual
- **Food / education:** Universal meals. Most compelling readily interpretable social-policy example; explicitly distinguish evaluated historical school-level provision from citywide launch.
- **Housing / justice:** Right to Counsel. Show a program-rollout estimate, not an inflated representation-complier estimate. Add a visible delivery-gap badge.
- **Air pollution / health:** Clean Heat. This is an adjusted spatial association, with a useful apartment-level null finding. Do not label it a climate-policy success on these data alone.
- **Mobility / air pollution:** Congestion pricing. Excellent demonstration that mobility gains and uncertain air-quality gains can coexist. The visual must show conflicting air results and explain different endpoints.
- **Optional health / income-work:** Paid sick leave. Useful care-access mechanism, but estimates are sensitive to specification and employment is unobserved.

## What the paper should argue
Policies connect to several Doughnut domains through mechanisms, but heterogeneous studies do not identify changes in a common Doughnut score. Keep three levels separate: policy deployment, evaluated outcome, and sufficiency/ceiling status. None of these studies establishes citywide entry into the safe-and-just space. A native-unit evidence card and an uncertainty-aware policy-to-slice network are more defensible than moving radial wedges by arbitrary percentages.

## Adversarial findings that must survive editing
1. Current housing service coverage is materially incomplete. High retention among represented households is selected descriptive evidence, not a causal effect.
2. Clean Heat's ambient-pollution study does not establish indoor exposure improvement. Gas substitution introduces an unmeasured life-cycle climate question. The fuel-oil program is not LL97 or NYS heat-pump subsidies.
3. Congestion pricing: NYC Health's August 2026 controlled evaluation reports no statistically significant attributable CRZ pollution difference, whereas Fraser et al. estimate a reduction in *daily maximum* PM2.5. Cook et al.'s August 2026 working paper also finds little air-quality effect. Avoid choosing only the positive number. October 2026 Goldberg et al. primary abstract adds a real-time-monitor DiD PM2.5 reduction, but no significant satellite-NO2 effect at 5%; full methods were not accessed. This strengthens the need to show air evidence as contested rather than choose a winner.
4. School meal study is middle-school exposure in 2010–2013. Its favorable learning/participation results cannot be automatically relabeled as the effect of 2017 universal citywide access.
5. Paid-leave study ED result is not robust to every specification; breast/cervical screening results are null. Its August 1 analytic cutoff differs from the official July 30 first-use date.

## Publication guardrails
- No summing across outcomes, populations or years; no arbitrary weights or normalized welfare units.
- Percentage points, percent, standard deviations, concentration and counts must stay visibly distinct.
- Separate fiscal transfer (tolls/reimbursements) from real resource costs (staff, food, equipment).
- Distribution is not a nuisance covariate: show who receives service, pays, waits, or is omitted.
- All historical evidence windows must be visible. Current program status does not make an old estimate current.
- Avoid claims of lives saved where the source is an exposure-response simulation, and avoid inferential upgrading of official press releases.

## Research access / residual limits
No external actions or publication occurred. Some publisher endpoints returned access errors; accessible author copies, official pages and primary full-text mirrors support the extracted cases. Congestion speed abstract is verified at the authors' university page, but its exact full sample date window was not obtained; the card should say '2025 rollout; August 2026 paper revision' rather than inventing a terminal date. All monetary net-benefit totals remain unestimated.
