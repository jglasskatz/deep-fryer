# Santa Cruz policy evidence — research memo

Research cutoff:26October2026. Santa Cruz,California is assumed.

## Bottom line

Five policies connect to several Doughnut slices. None of the reviewed local studies identifies a causal change in a whole Doughnut outcome. Separate outputs (permits,food tonnage),behavior(reported transit use),resource demand(water),and accounting intensities(electricity). No aggregate success score is justified.

## Boundary rules

Keep City,unincorporated County,City Water service area,METRO and3CE separate. Slice mappings indicate plausible pathways,not measured threshold closure.

## Drought rationing and water-waste restrictions

**Boundary:** City of Santa Cruz Water Department service area — Utility service area; not city boundary or whole county

**Headline:** 26% lower water use (June–November 2014 vs June–November 2013).

**Evidence:** Descriptive. Supplier administrative reports summarized by County HSA; table visually checked on PDF page 13.

Official Attachment 2 reports 26% average monthly savings, alongside residential use of 47 gallons/person/day. Cover text says 28% without matching window; use the explicitly windowed table, not cover figure.

**Identification:** Prior year only, not weather-adjusted untreated comparison. Service-area result, not city resident effect. 26% is average monthly savings, not necessarily volume-weighted seasonal savings. No untreated counterfactual. No measured ecological threshold closure.

**Costs/distribution:** Program operating cost not isolated. Contemporary reporting documents utility revenue pressure as reduced sales leave infrastructure costs; no cost-effectiveness estimate. Penalties and fixed revenue-recovery charges may burden low-income households; marginal conservation scope differs between efficient households and heavy outdoor users. These distributional mechanisms are not locally quantified.

**Spillovers:** Demand reductions protect scarce supply but do not establish ecological streamflow benefits or household wellbeing changes. Drought, publicity, restrictions and price responses cannot be separated.

## ADU permitting reforms and fee/design assistance

**Boundary:** Unincorporated Santa Cruz County; California enabling/preemptive laws — Unincorporated county, excludes City of Santa Cruz

**Headline:** 28 → 103 ADU permits (2017 vs 2024).

**Evidence:** Descriptive. County administrative annual permit series.

2024 General Plan Annual Report Table1:2017 28;2018 37;2019 29;2020 37;2021 63;2022 58;2023 85;2024 103.2024 includes one tiny home on wheels classified as an ADU. This is a permitting output, not completed/occupied units or rent reduction.

**Identification:** No untreated jurisdiction or modeled no-reform trajectory. State/local policy bundle cannot be separated from demand, credit and construction conditions. Use February2025 General Plan report Table1 full-year series; source explicitly includes one tiny home on wheels among2024 ADUs.

**Costs/distribution:** Report identifies construction and permit costs as barriers; fee reductions and free designs are supports. Total fiscal cost not established. Forgivable Loan Program ended in 2022 for low performance. Benefits require property/financing access; potential additional rental or family space is not guaranteed affordable. County removed rent restrictions outside Coastal Zone in2008 and inside in2009.

**Spillovers:** Infill can reduce land pressure relative to sprawl but adds water, materials and infrastructure demand. No lifecycle or rent effect measured.

## Youth Cruz Free fares

**Boundary:** Santa Cruz Metropolitan Transit District — Regional county-serving transit authority, not City policy

**Headline:** 31% of respondents started riding (Recalled pre-March2023 vs during pilot, survey2024).

**Evidence:** Descriptive. UC Davis observational survey:394 usable cases overall;280 for Table1. School/network recruitment, not representative probability sample..

86/280 respondents reported riding only after pilot began; 136/280 rode in both periods; 33/280 stopped;25/280 never rode. Agency also reported536% youth ridership growth, but accessible summary lacks raw denominator/time window, so do not use that as the main metric.

**Identification:** None; nonrandom retrospective survey. Recall/selection bias, no control group, pandemic recovery and service changes. 31% is new rider share, not ridership growth. Age-related driving access changes travel behavior.

**Costs/distribution:** December2024 board report records $1,192,777 LCTOP award for continuation of free/reduced fares, with confirmation of Youth Cruz Free funding being pursued. Grant award is not total program cost or expenditure. Fare removal supports youth without cars; survey associations with ethnicity, vehicle access and safety suggest benefits/access are uneven. Rural and communities-of-color representation requires improvement.

**Spillovers:** Fare-free service requires stable replacement funding and sufficient service. New bus trips need not replace car trips; cannot infer CO2 savings. No causal school-attendance result.

**Sources:**

- [Pike et al. Youth Cruz Free, UC Davis/NCST August2025, Table1 p10](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/86231/dot_86231_DS1.pdf) — Primary research

## Source-separated food-scrap collection

**Boundary:** City of Santa Cruz implementing California AB1826 and SB1383 — City solid-waste collection customers only

**Headline:** About40 tons diverted weekly (City2023 newsletter, residential+commercial).

**Evidence:** Descriptive. Municipal operational throughput reporting.

FY2018 report:45–50 tons/month from commercial collection serving over26 businesses. City2023 newsletter:about40 tons/week from residential+commercial. Changed service population means these are rollout milestones, not like-for-like efficacy estimates.

**Identification:** No measured no-program disposal trajectory; city's diverted-from-landfill accounting. No measured methane avoided, lifecycle emissions, food security improvement or nutrient runoff effect. Do not annualize40tons/week as observed annual tonnage or confuse with county's broader organics totals.

**Costs/distribution:** Capital/operating cost not established from primary sources reviewed. Do not calculate net fiscal savings from throughput. Rollout newsletter says multifamily complexes not yet enrolled could use drop-off; convenience differed by housing type. Current mandatory service applies all housing types.

**Spillovers:** Transport and processing consume energy; feedstock recovery is not edible food redistribution. Biogas at City's wastewater plant was future work in2023, not a demonstrated benefit then.

## Community-choice electricity procurement (3CE)

**Boundary:** Central Coast Community Energy multi-jurisdiction JPA — Regional electricity supplier; not a Santa Cruz-only outcome

**Headline:** 551 vs384 lbCO2e/MWh (2025, 3CE total vs CA utility-average total, same scope).

**Evidence:** Descriptive + modeled. CEC power-source disclosure accounting; joint3CE/PG&E tariff illustration.

2025 label: 3CE total intensity551 vs California utility-average total384 lbCO2e/MWh (retail sales, other end uses and losses). Retail-only3Cchoice542;3Cprime0. These are accounting intensities, not marginal or causal avoided emissions.

**Identification:** No causal no-CCA comparison. Tariff illustration compares matched375kWh usage at published2024 rates.. Do not use regional energy totals as Santa Cruz city effects.2025 new reporting separates retail-only and total scopes:compare total551 with total384, not retail542 with state-total384. No automatic climate-success label.

**Costs/distribution:** 2024 E-TOU-C illustration at375kWh/month:PG&E$172.57 vs3Cchoice$158.71, modeled$13.86/month (~8.0%) difference including delivery/PCIA. Not measured household savings, not current2026 rates, and rate effective dates differ (PG&EJune1;3CEJan1). Benefits vary with tariff, usage, vintage fees and eligibility. Cheap generation alone does not remove delivery charges. No local energy-poverty effect identified.

**Spillovers:** New renewable contracts can have additionality benefits but procurement accounting does not prove them. Offsite land/material and grid effects require lifecycle evidence. A100%renewable opt-up product is not default service.
