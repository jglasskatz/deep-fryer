# Monterey County, California — region brief

**Scope.** This portrait covers Monterey County as a countywide jurisdiction: all territory within the county boundary, including its incorporated cities and unincorporated areas. It does not mean the City of Monterey. The Census Bureau's 2020 PUMA reference map identifies Monterey County as California county 053 (GEOID 06053). ([2020 Census Monterey County PUMA map](https://www2.census.gov/geo/maps/DC2020/PUMA/st06_ca/DC20PUMA_0605301.pdf))

## Geography and boundaries

- Monterey County has twelve incorporated cities: Carmel-by-the-Sea, Del Rey Oaks, Gonzales, Greenfield, King City, Marina, Monterey, Pacific Grove, Salinas, Sand City, Seaside, and Soledad. The State Controller's county listing names these municipalities; Monterey County's LAFCO city-limits GIS service says its city boundaries are determined by LAFCO and that annexations or city-limit changes require LAFCO approval. ([California State Controller, cities in Monterey County](https://gcc.sco.ca.gov/Reports/Cities/CitiesByCounty.aspx?county=Monterey&year=2023); [Monterey County LAFCO city-limits layer](https://maps.co.monterey.ca.us/server/rest/services/LAFCO/City_Limits/MapServer/info/iteminfo))
- The County's land-use map distinguishes incorporated cities from unincorporated communities and labels the county boundary. It includes communities such as Pajaro, Castroville, Prunedale, Moss Landing, Spreckels, Chualar, Carmel Valley Village, Big Sur, San Lucas, San Ardo, Lockwood, and Bradley. The map says its road data came from County IT/GIS and city limits/spheres of influence from Monterey County LAFCO. ([Monterey County incorporated-cities map](https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/45906/639117785515670000))
- Countywide data and regional data are different footprints. AMBAG's regional area is Monterey, San Benito, and Santa Cruz counties; Monterey Bay Air Resources District (MBARD) serves those same three counties. Monterey One Water describes a northern Monterey County wastewater service area, while the Monterey Peninsula Water Management District (MPWMD) publishes water reporting for its district. These service and planning areas should not be treated as coterminous with the county. ([AMBAG About Us](https://www.ambag.org/about-us); [MBARD air quality and planning](https://www.mbard.org/air-quality-and-planning); [Monterey One Water About](https://www.montereyonewater.org/31/About); [MPWMD annual reports](https://www.mpwmd.net/resources/annual-reports/))

## Governance and data owners

- The County's public climate-plan board report records coordination among County Housing and Community Development, Public Works, Emergency Management, the Agricultural Commissioner, Health, the Monterey County Water Resources Agency (MCWRA), and Economic Development, as well as external partners including TAMC, Monterey-Salinas Transit, AMBAG, the Resource Conservation District, CSU Monterey Bay, and UC Cooperative Extension. This is a useful map of local and regional data owners, not a statement that each agency's data uses countywide geography. ([Monterey County Board report on the Community Climate Action and Adaptation Plan](https://monterey.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?FullText=1&GUID=F6F00ECF-57EF-4163-B7E1-81DC2FAD2479&ID=8013107))
- Monterey County LAFCO maintains city boundary mapping; its GIS city-limits layer identifies the limits as LAFCO-defined. ([LAFCO City Limits layer](https://maps.co.monterey.ca.us/server/rest/services/LAFCO/City_Limits/MapServer/info/iteminfo))
- AMBAG is a joint-powers regional organization and federally designated MPO/COG for Monterey, San Benito, and Santa Cruz counties. It manages a regional travel-demand model and prepares regional housing, population, and employment forecasts. Forecasts and regional plans provide context but are not county observations unless the county-specific row is used. ([AMBAG About Us](https://www.ambag.org/about-us); [AMBAG Regional Growth Forecast](https://www.ambag.org/plans/regional-growth-forecast))

## Data portals, plans, and recurring publications

- **County plans and reporting:** the County is developing a Community Climate Action and Adaptation Plan; the Board report is a public-draft review and comment item, so it should not be described as adopted based on that record. ([CCAAP Board report](https://monterey.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?FullText=1&GUID=F6F00ECF-57EF-4163-B7E1-81DC2FAD2479&ID=8013107))
- **Groundwater:** MCWRA and the Salinas Valley Basin Groundwater Sustainability Agency (SVBGSA) operate a Groundwater Monitoring Program covering well registration, groundwater elevation and quality monitoring, and extraction monitoring. SVBGSA publishes annual reports and sustainability plans by subbasin; it states that public reports aggregate extraction data while personally identifiable information is restricted. Its coverage follows basin/subbasin boundaries and is not necessarily the whole county. ([SVBGSA Groundwater Monitoring Program](https://svbgsa.org/gmp/); [SVBGSA Monterey Subbasin reports](https://svbgsa.org/monterey-subbasin/); [SVBGSA information library](https://svbgsa.org/resources/information-library/))
- **Peninsula water:** MPWMD publishes annual reports, water-production reports, and surface-water data. Its report catalog is a district source; confirm its district/service-area coverage before using any value as a county total. ([MPWMD Annual Reports](https://www.mpwmd.net/resources/annual-reports/); [MPWMD water production report, WY 2024](https://www.mpwmd.net/asd/board/boardpacket/2025/04212025/08/Item-8.htm); [MPWMD surface-water data portal](https://map.mpwmd.net/))
- **Wastewater and reuse:** Monterey One Water publishes service-area and financial materials. It describes itself as the sewer-treatment provider in northern Monterey County and its annual financial report documents wastewater treatment and recycled-water work with MCWRA and MPWMD. This footprint is partial county coverage. ([Monterey One Water About](https://www.montereyonewater.org/31/About); [Monterey One Water FY 2022–23 Popular Annual Financial Report](https://www.montereyonewater.org/Archive/ViewFile/Item/65))
- **Air and transportation:** MBARD operates ambient monitoring and submits data to EPA and CARB across the North Central Coast Air Basin, spanning Monterey, Santa Cruz, and San Benito counties. TAMC posts recurring annual reports and develops a Monterey County Regional Transportation Plan; TAMC says that plan includes priorities from all twelve cities and the County. ([MBARD Air Quality and Planning](https://www.mbard.org/air-quality-and-planning); [TAMC Annual Reports](https://www.tamcmonterey.org/tamc-annual-report); [TAMC Regional Transportation Plan](https://www.tamcmonterey.org/regional-transportation-plan))

## Institutions and service footprints

- **Hospitals:** Monterey County's hospital directory lists Community Hospital of the Monterey Peninsula, Natividad Medical Center, Salinas Valley Memorial Healthcare System, and George L. Mee Memorial Hospital. Natividad separately identifies itself as County-owned and operated and says it serves Monterey County and the Central Coast. ([County Hospitals directory](https://www.countyofmonterey.gov/Home/Components/ServiceDirectory/ServiceDirectory/1913/1334); [Natividad](https://www.natividad.com/))
- **Higher education and research:** Hartnell College's institutional profile says it serves the Salinas Valley and southern San Benito County, draws students from Monterey Peninsula communities, and partners with CSU Monterey Bay and research institutions. This describes an institution's service and student reach, not a county-resident-only population. ([Hartnell College About](https://www.hartnell.edu/about/index.html))
- **Regional environmental and public-service institutions:** MBARD, AMBAG, MPWMD, MCWRA/SVBGSA, Monterey One Water, TAMC, and the County's LAFCO are relevant public data owners; each publishes for its own basin, district, service area, or regional footprint described above. ([MBARD](https://www.mbard.org/air-quality-and-planning); [AMBAG](https://www.ambag.org/about-us); [MPWMD](https://www.mpwmd.net/resources/annual-reports/); [SVBGSA](https://svbgsa.org/resources/information-library/); [Monterey One Water](https://www.montereyonewater.org/31/About); [TAMC](https://www.tamcmonterey.org/regional-transportation-plan); [Monterey County LAFCO](https://maps.co.monterey.ca.us/server/rest/services/LAFCO/City_Limits/MapServer/info/iteminfo))

## Statistical geographies and cautions

- **County and places:** Census county GEOID 06053 is the direct countywide geography. Census ACS geographic products include county, county-subdivision, tract, and place levels. Monterey County's twelve cities can be analyzed as Census places, but place estimates alone omit the county's unincorporated remainder; do not treat the county as the City of Monterey or sum city values without checking ACS universe, vintage, margins of error, and boundary alignment. ([Census Monterey County map](https://www2.census.gov/geo/maps/DC2020/PUMA/st06_ca/DC20PUMA_0605301.pdf); [2024 ACS geography levels](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs/acs5/profile/geography.html); [State Controller city list](https://gcc.sco.ca.gov/Reports/Cities/CitiesByCounty.aspx?county=Monterey&year=2023))
- **PUMAs:** the 2020 Census delineates three relevant PUMAs: 0605301 (north-central Monterey County), 0605302 (northeast Monterey County/Salinas), and 0605303 (southern/eastern Monterey County **and San Benito County**). PUMAs are statistical areas sized for PUMS/ACS microdata, not jurisdiction boundaries; 0605303 is explicitly cross-county, and none of these three should be used as a county-only substitute without a documented method. ([Census 2020 PUMA map catalog](https://www2.census.gov/geo/maps/DC2020/PUMA/st06_ca/Catalog_PUMAmaps_st06.pdf); [Census PUMA overview](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/geography/guidance/geo-areas/pumas.html))
- **Tracts and blocks:** Census says tracts nest within PUMAs and the PUMA boundaries are for statistical tabulation, not jurisdictional authority. Tract/block analysis needs a current county-boundary overlay and a documented allocation method, especially for partial boundary overlaps. ([Census PUMA geography guidance](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/geography/guidance/geo-areas/pumas.html); [Census Monterey County PUMA map](https://www2.census.gov/geo/maps/DC2020/PUMA/st06_ca/DC20PUMA_0605301.pdf))
- **Framework coverage:** the local contract defines 12 social and 9 ecological dimensions, spanning measures such as health, housing, education, water, energy, climate, biodiversity, and air pollution. This brief establishes jurisdiction and source footprints only; it does not report indicator findings. ([Local framework contract](../../../../packages/contract/framework/framework.json))
